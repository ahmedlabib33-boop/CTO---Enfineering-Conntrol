# USER CHOICE FOR EVERY UNRESOLVED CONFLICT

For every unresolved conflict that materially affects schedule development, the Analyzer must **ask the user to choose the resolution**.

The Analyzer must provide a clear **three-choice question**.

Example structure:

### Conflict C-001

**Issue:**
Contract requires 14 days while another project document indicates 7 days.

**Source 1:** Contract — Clause XX — Page XX
**Source 2:** Specification — Section XX — Page XX

**Schedule Impact:** Approval-cycle duration changes by 7 days.

### Choose:

**Option 1 — Apply Source 1**
Use the Contract requirement of 14 days.
Schedule consequence: approval activities will use 14 days.

**Option 2 — Apply Source 2**
Use the Specification requirement of 7 days.
Schedule consequence: approval activities will use 7 days.

**Option 3 — Keep Unresolved / Request Formal Clarification**
Do not select either value yet. Maintain the item as conflicting until an RFI, Engineer instruction, Employer clarification, or other formal resolution is obtained.

The exact choices must be dynamically generated according to the actual conflict.

The choices must be **evidence-based**.

The Analyzer must not create fake alternatives merely to produce three options.

Where appropriate, the options may include:

* Follow contractual hierarchy
* Follow latest approved revision
* Follow specific source
* Request formal clarification
* Raise RFI
* Obtain Engineer decision
* Obtain Employer decision
* Keep unresolved

# AACE RP 38R-06 — Schedule Basis Intelligence for a Primavera P6 / XER Engine

## Source Boundary

This knowledge file is derived from the uploaded AACE International Recommended Practice No. 38R-06, **Documenting the Schedule Basis** (2009).

The RP is a **guideline**, not a mandatory standard. Its purpose is to define the information that may be documented as the basis, assumptions, methods, interfaces, risks, exclusions, and controls behind a project schedule.

This file converts those concepts into an implementable planning-engine specification. It does not reproduce the source document and does not create contractual requirements that are not present in the governing contract.

---

## 1. Core Meaning

A Primavera schedule should not exist as an unexplained network of activities and dates.

The schedule engine should be able to explain:

- what the project is;
- what scope is included and excluded;
- how the schedule was integrated;
- what execution strategy was selected;
- what dates and milestones control the plan;
- how durations were developed;
- what calendars were used;
- how logic and sequencing were determined;
- what production-rate sources were used;
- what resources and crew assumptions were used;
- how engineering, procurement, vendors, third parties, and commissioning interface with construction;
- what critical and near-critical paths exist;
- what risks, assumptions, exclusions, exceptions, and reserves affect the forecast;
- what software rules, leads/lags, constraints, float rules, calendars, and coding structures were applied;
- who reviewed and accepted the planning basis.

The schedule basis should be treated as a **life-cycle record** linked to the schedule revision it explains.

# AACE RP 48R-06 — Schedule Constructability Intelligence for a Primavera P6 / XER Engine

## Source Boundary

This knowledge file is derived from the uploaded AACE International Recommended Practice No. 48R-06, **Schedule Constructability Review** (2009).

The RP is a **guideline**, not a mandatory standard. It describes a constructability-oriented review of the construction schedule and the execution plan represented by that schedule.

This file converts the source concepts into implementable schedule-review logic for a Primavera P6 / XER creation engine. It does not reproduce the source document and does not create contract obligations not found in the governing project documents.

---

## 1. Core Meaning

A construction schedule should represent a plan that is:

- comprehensive;
- realistic;
- buildable;
- coordinated;
- logically sequenced;
- supported by procurement;
- supported by engineering;
- compatible with site conditions;
- compatible with available labor/equipment/material;
- integrated with testing, startup, commissioning, turnover, and demobilization.

The constructability engine asks a different question from a normal CPM validator.

A CPM validator asks:

> Is the network mathematically schedulable?

A constructability reviewer asks:

> Can the project actually be built this way?

Both are required.

---

## 2. Constructability Review Objectives

The engine should test whether the schedule is:

- consistent with scope;
- clear;
- complete;
- reasonable in sequence;
- coordinated across engineering disciplines and craft trades;
- integrated with startup and commissioning;
- supported by realistic procurement lead times;
- feasible within site access and work restrictions.

It should identify:

- out-of-sequence work;
- missing work;
- unrealistic durations;
- missing interfaces;
- procurement conflicts;
- engineering-release conflicts;
- access conflicts;
- congestion;
- resource peaks;
- equipment limitations;
- premature or late material delivery;
- incomplete handover/startup integration.


A conceptual schedule may contain only major milestones.

An execution schedule may require hundreds or thousands of activities depending on project scale.

The engine should not reject a conceptual schedule for lacking execution-level detail, but it should evaluate the schedule against its declared phase.

- craft demand by time;

## 20. Optimum Sequencing

The project plan should be **construction driven**.

Engineering and procurement should support construction need dates.

Constructability logic:

```text
Construction Need
← Engineering Release
← Procurement / Delivery
```

not merely:

```text
Engineering finishes
→ Procurement finishes
→ construction starts whenever those finish
```

The backward need-date check is essential.

---

## 21. Civil / Underground Sequencing

The review should consider early completion of underground and drainage works when practical.

Potential sequence:

```text
Utility Detection
→ Excavation
→ Storm/Sewer/Underground Utilities
→ Testing
→ Backfill
→ Compaction
→ Road / Hardscape
```

Flag final roads/hardscape scheduled before incomplete underground services unless a justified phased strategy exists.

Where contractually permitted, permanent systems may support construction.

Examples can include:

- lighting;
- transformers/panels;
- water;
- compressed air;
- mechanical services.

The engine should check:

- permission;
- warranty implications;
- vendor support;
- protection;
- temporary load;
- commissioning status;
- handback/reverification.

## 28. Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Constructability review evaluates the **execution plan represented by the schedule**, not only the file structure.
2. The schedule should cover the project lifecycle from start/mobilization through demobilization.
3. Engineering and procurement should support construction need dates.
4. Long-lead durations require a source.
5. Site access, logistics, storage, and equipment routing can make mathematically valid logic unbuildable.
6. Weather and work-hour restrictions belong in the planning basis/calendars where appropriate.
7. Work by others and third-party interfaces must be visible.
8. Resource peaks and equipment limitations must be reviewed.
9. Testing/startup/commissioning must be integrated, not appended at the end.
10. Detail should be appropriate to the schedule's phase and control purpose.
11. Constructability findings are review outputs and must not silently alter the raw/source schedule.
12. Native Primavera P6 scheduling remains the final CPM authority after XER import.

## 29. Engine Outcome

A constructability-enabled XER creator should be able to answer:

- Is this sequence physically buildable?
- Are engineering releases early enough?
- Are procurement durations realistic and sourced?
- Are materials arriving when needed?
- Can the site receive/store the equipment?
- Is access preserved for cranes/trucks/crews?
- Do underground works precede permanent surfaces where required?
- Are resource peaks feasible?
- Are weather-sensitive activities protected?
- Is the building enclosed before sensitive finishes where required?
- Are work-by-others interfaces shown?
- Are testing and commissioning integrated?
- Are temporary works removed in time for final work?
- Is demobilization represented?

If those answers are not supported, the engine should flag the issue before the schedule is treated as baseline-ready.

Core Preservation & Data Contract

These rules control how every future importer, agent and application handles evidence and derived information.

CORE DATA CONTRACT

1. Original source evidence is immutable.
2. Normalized information is stored separately.
3. Project selections/overrides never overwrite source values.
4. One master activity may map to unlimited source activities.
5. One activity may have unlimited productivity options.
6. One activity may have unlimited crew alternatives.
7. Crew constituents are individual structured resource rows, not one text string.
8. Equipment constituents are structured rows with quantity and capacity.
9. Materials are structured by primary/secondary/consumable/temporary/accessory.
10. Excel formulas are preserved exactly in addition to evaluated results.
11. NULL is different from zero.
12. Conflicting productivity is compared, not averaged.
13. Rate basis must be validated before man-hour conversion.
14. Federal references are secondary fallback sources, not replacements for project evidence.
15. UFGS technical requirements do not automatically provide productivity.
16. MII/MCACES crew/task productivity is used only when the actual task is identifiable.
17. Engineering-derived information must be labeled as such.
18. Every editable record has revision history.
19. Every calculated result stores its formula and input record IDs.
20. Every activity supports upstream engineering/procurement and downstream inspection/T&C/as-built/handover links.
